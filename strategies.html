01

Foundational protection

The core strategies most people start with — making sure the people who depend on your income are covered.

Related products:Term life insuranceWhole life insuranceGuaranteed insurability rider

Term life for income replacement

The lowest-cost way to replace your income for a set number of years — sized to the actual gap your family would face, not a generic multiple of salary.

Return-of-premium term

A term policy that refunds your premiums if you outlive the term — costs more, but nothing is "lost" if you never make a claim.

Convertible term

Lets you convert some or all of a term policy to permanent coverage later, without new medical underwriting — useful if your health changes.

Laddering multiple term policies

Stacking policies of different lengths so coverage steps down as needs shrink — for example, a 20-year policy sized to the mortgage and a 10-year policy sized to the years until kids are independent.

Mortgage protection coverage

Term coverage sized and timed specifically to pay off the remaining mortgage balance if something happens to you.

Supplementing employer group life

Group life through work is usually cheap but small and not portable if you leave the job — most people need individual coverage alongside it, not instead of it.

02

Living benefits & riders

Optional add-ons that extend what a policy covers, often for a modest additional cost.

Related products:All ridersLong-term care rider

Accidental Death Benefit (ADB)

Pays an additional amount on top of the base death benefit if death results from a covered accident.

Waiver of Premium

Waives future premiums if you become totally disabled, so the policy — and any cash value growth — stays in force without you having to keep paying.

Chronic illness / long-term care acceleration

Lets you access part of your death benefit while living if you're diagnosed with a qualifying chronic or terminal illness.

Child term rider

Adds a flat amount of coverage on your children under one rider, instead of buying separate policies for each child.

Guaranteed insurability rider

Lets you buy additional coverage at set future dates or life events without new medical underwriting — valuable if your health may decline.

03

Cash value & liquidity strategies

Ways permanent policies can function as a flexible pool of money while you are alive, not just a death payout.

Related products:Whole life insuranceUniversal life insuranceIndexed universal life

Policy loans against cash value

Borrow against accumulated cash value for an opportunity or emergency, generally without a credit check or bank underwriting. Loans accrue interest and reduce the death benefit while outstanding.

Using a policy as collateral

Assign a policy's cash value as collateral to help secure a business loan or real estate financing, sometimes on better terms than an unsecured loan.

"Infinite Banking" / Bank-on-Yourself style strategies

Using a properly structured whole life policy as your own source of financing — borrowing against it and repaying yourself instead of a bank, while cash value keeps compounding. Requires discipline and a long horizon to work as advertised.

Cash value as an emergency-fund backstop

A supplemental layer of liquidity behind a traditional emergency fund, for when unexpected costs exceed what is in savings.

1035 exchange

Moves cash value from an old or underperforming policy into a new one without triggering current income tax on the gain.

04

Retirement & tax-advantaged growth

Using permanent insurance as a supplemental — not primary — retirement savings vehicle.

Related products:Indexed universal lifeWhole life insuranceIncome annuitiesInvestment advisory services

Life Insurance Retirement Plan (LIRP)

An overfunded whole life or IUL policy designed to generate supplemental, tax-advantaged income in retirement through policy loans.

Whole life dividend options

Choosing how dividends are used — buying more paid-up coverage, reducing premiums, accumulating at interest, or taking them as cash — to match your goals. Dividends are not guaranteed.

Avoiding Modified Endowment Contract (MEC) status

Keeping premium funding within IRS limits so loans and withdrawals keep their favorable tax treatment instead of being taxed like an annuity.

IUL vs. VUL trade-offs

Indexed universal life credits growth linked to a market index with a floor and a cap; variable universal life invests directly in sub-accounts with full market risk and reward. Choosing between them is a risk-tolerance decision.

Roth conversions paired with a death benefit

Converting pre-tax balances in low-income "gap years" while using life insurance as the tax-free legacy asset — particularly relevant since the SECURE Act ended the stretch IRA for most non-spouse heirs.

05

Business owner strategies

Protecting a business, its owners, and its key people using life insurance as the funding mechanism.

Related products:Whole life insuranceDisability insuranceBusiness planning services

Buy-sell agreements

Funds a pre-agreed buyout if an owner dies — structured as cross-purchase (owners insure each other) or entity-purchase (the business owns the policies). The choice has real tax and basis consequences.

Key person insurance

Protects the business financially if a critical owner or employee dies, covering the cost and disruption of replacing them. Employer-owned policies have notice and consent requirements.

Executive bonus plans (Section 162)

The business pays premiums on a policy owned by a key employee as a bonus, a simple way to help retain top talent without a complex plan.

Split-dollar arrangements

The business and an executive share the costs and benefits of a policy under a written agreement — often used for executive retention.

Cash value as business collateral

Accumulated cash value in a business-owned policy can support a line of credit for growth without taking on new outside debt.

Disability buy-out and overhead coverage

Funds a buyout if an owner becomes permanently disabled, and reimburses fixed business costs during a shorter absence — the triggers most agreements name but never fund.

06

Estate & wealth transfer

Using life insurance to move wealth to the next generation efficiently and provide liquidity when it is needed most.

Related products:Survivorship life insuranceEstate planning coordination

Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT)

Holds a policy outside your taxable estate, so the death benefit isn't added to your estate's value for tax purposes. Requires careful drafting, gifting mechanics, and a real trustee.

Survivorship (second-to-die) life insurance

Covers two people (often spouses) and pays out after the second death — commonly used to provide liquidity for estate taxes, at a lower cost than two individual policies.

Equalizing inheritances

Uses a policy payout to give one heir a comparable inheritance when another heir receives an illiquid asset, like a family business or property.

Wealth replacement trusts

Pairs a charitable gift of an asset with a life insurance policy that replaces that asset's value for your heirs.

Charitable beneficiary designations

Naming a charity or donor-advised fund as a full or partial policy beneficiary as part of a giving strategy.

Planning for a non-citizen spouse

Property passing to a non-US-citizen spouse does not qualify for the unlimited marital deduction. A qualified domestic trust (QDOT) defers rather than eliminates the tax, which creates a liquidity need.

07

Advanced & high-net-worth strategies

More complex structures generally used by high-net-worth individuals and businesses, usually alongside an attorney or CPA.

Related products:Variable universal lifePrivate wealth management

Premium financing

Borrowing from a bank (not the policy) to pay premiums on a large policy, preserving other assets and liquidity — carries interest-rate and collateral risk that needs careful structuring and honest downside modelling.

Private placement life insurance

A specialized policy structure for accredited investors seeking tax-efficient growth on alternative investments held inside the policy.

Life insurance in divorce settlements

Securing alimony or child support obligations by requiring the paying spouse to maintain a policy naming the other spouse or children as beneficiary.

Pre-liquidity-event trust funding

Gifting closely held or pre-IPO interests into an irrevocable trust while the valuation is low, so future appreciation grows outside the taxable estate.

08

Life-stage review triggers

Points in life worth revisiting your coverage and beneficiaries, even if nothing feels urgent.

Related products:Additional Purchase Benefit riderRetirement planning

Major life events

Marriage, a new child, a new home, starting a business, or a significant promotion are all good moments to re-check whether your coverage still matches your actual needs.

Beneficiary reviews

Divorce, remarriage, or a death in the family are reasons to confirm your beneficiary designations are still correct — outdated designations are one of the most common estate-planning mistakes.

Term conversion deadlines

Most term policies include a right to convert to permanent coverage without new underwriting, and that right expires at a stated age or duration. Check the contract, not your memory.

Changing jobs or leaving an employer

Group life and group disability end with employment, often with a short conversion window. The time to review is before you give notice.
