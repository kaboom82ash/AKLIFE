For professional athletes, performers, creators and commission earners

You will earn in eight years what most people earn in forty. The plan has to reflect that.

Compressed earning windows change every assumption in conventional financial advice. The risk is not a bad market — it is a career that ends abruptly, a lifestyle built on peak income, and no plan for the thirty years after the money stops.

  • A forced-savings structure funded during peak years, not after them
  • Disability and loss-of-value coverage for a career-ending event

The real problem

What a compressed earning window actually does

The arithmetic is unforgiving. A career that produces most of its lifetime income in under a decade has to fund four decades of life, and every dollar spent in the peak years is a dollar not compounding across the rest.

The career can end in one play, or one season

A single injury, a vocal cord issue, a repetitive strain injury, an industry contraction. There is no notice period and no severance.

Guaranteed money and total money are not the same

Announced contract values frequently include non-guaranteed years, incentives and option seasons. Planning against the headline number is planning against a figure you may never receive.

Lifestyle sets at peak income

Housing, vehicles, travel and the people who depend on you all calibrate to the best year. Those commitments do not scale down gracefully.

You support more people than you list on a form

Parents, siblings, extended family and a small staff. Many of those obligations are informal, permanent, and would not survive your income stopping.

Very high income, very short tax planning window

Several years in the top bracket, often in multiple state jurisdictions, with limited access to conventional employer retirement plans.

Advisors turn over

Agents, business managers and advisors change frequently in these industries. Structures that require constant active management tend to be the first thing to break.

Publicity and probate do not mix

An estate that passes through probate becomes a public record, which is a genuine problem for anyone whose name appears in headlines.

Young, wealthy and without documents

A 24-year-old with eight figures and no will, no beneficiary designations and no guardianship provisions is an entirely normal situation in this cohort.

How the tools get used

Turning peak years into a floor you cannot outlive

The goal is to convert a short window of extraordinary income into predictable lifetime cash flow, with protection against the events that end the window early.

Do this first

Disability and loss-of-value coverage

Specialty markets underwrite career-ending disability for athletes and performers, and in some cases loss-of-value coverage that responds if an injury materially reduces a future contract. These are specialised placements with real underwriting, exclusions and documentation requirements — the details determine whether a claim pays.

Why it fits: For an athlete, this is the coverage most directly aimed at the actual risk. Read the definitions closely, or have someone who will.
Structure

Fund permanent coverage heavily during the peak years

An overfunded permanent policy, structured to stay within IRS premium limits, grows cash value on a tax-deferred basis and can later provide income through policy loans. Structurally, it is a commitment device: money moved into a policy is much harder to spend impulsively than money in a brokerage account.

Why it fits: The behavioural benefit is not a footnote here. For this cohort it is frequently the main benefit.
Income floor

An annuity to create income you cannot outlive

Converting a portion of peak earnings into a contractual income stream, beginning either immediately or at a chosen future date, produces a floor that is independent of markets, decisions, and whoever is advising you in fifteen years.

Why it fits: The case for a floor is strongest exactly where future earning capacity is most uncertain.
Protect the people

Coverage for the family who depends on your income

Parents, children and others supported informally have no legal claim on your estate unless you create one. A policy with clear beneficiary designations is the simplest way to make sure support continues.

Why it fits: Informal support obligations are the most commonly overlooked exposure in this cohort by a wide margin.
Privacy

Transfer that avoids probate and the public record

A life insurance death benefit paid to a named beneficiary or a trust passes outside probate. That means faster payment, no public filing of the estate inventory, and far less exposure to opportunistic claims.

Why it fits: For a public figure, privacy is not a soft benefit. It is protection against a specific and well-documented pattern of estate litigation.
Documents

The basics, done at 24 rather than 44

A will, powers of attorney, healthcare directives, guardianship provisions for minor children, and current beneficiary designations on every account. Unglamorous, quick, and the thing that most often turns out to be missing.

Why it fits: The estate disasters that make the news in this industry are almost always about missing documents, not sophisticated tax planning.
After the career

Planning the second act deliberately

Most people in this cohort will work again — coaching, broadcasting, a business, an ownership stake. Planning that assumes zero future income is too pessimistic; planning that assumes replacement income is too optimistic. We build the floor first and treat the second act as upside.

Why it fits: A floor that covers your fixed obligations changes what you can afford to try next.
Caution

Be sceptical of complex structures

This cohort attracts complicated proposals — premium financing, private placement products, exotic tax structures. Some are legitimate for the right situation. Many are sold on illustrations that assume everything goes well. We will show you the unfavourable scenario for anything we propose, and we will tell you when the simple answer is better.

Why it fits: If a strategy cannot be explained in plain language, that is information about the strategy.

Illustrative examples

What this looks like in practice

Hypothetical situations built to show how the pieces fit together. Yours will be different — that is the point of the call.

The professional athlete

Building a floor during the guaranteed years

Situation
Age 25, three years into a professional career, two guaranteed years remaining on the current contract. Supporting two parents and a younger sibling. No estate documents, no disability coverage beyond what the league provides.
Strategy
Career-ending disability coverage placed through a specialty market, a permanent policy funded heavily across the guaranteed years, a deferred income annuity beginning at 45, and basic estate documents with beneficiary designations naming a trust.
If the career ends early, disability coverage responds and the accumulated cash value is still there. If it runs long, the deferred annuity begins paying at exactly the age when most former athletes discover their income has ended and their expenses have not. Either way, family support does not depend on the next contract.
The touring performer

Volatile income, permanent obligations

Situation
Age 38, income between 400,000 and 2.1 million a year depending on the touring cycle. Two children from a previous relationship with a support obligation running to age 22. Catalogue and royalty income that continues but is difficult to value.
Strategy
A term policy sized to secure the support obligation with the children's trust as beneficiary, permanent coverage funded in strong years and structured to allow flexible premiums in weak ones, and disability coverage reflecting the specific occupational risk.
The support obligation is secured by a death benefit rather than by a promise, so it survives regardless of what happens to touring income. The flexible permanent structure absorbs a light year without lapsing, which is what usually kills policies bought on peak-year assumptions.

Straight answers

Questions we get from athletes & entertainers

My team or my union already provides benefits. Is that enough?

It is a useful base and it is almost never sufficient on its own. League and union benefits are typically capped, often tied to years of service, and they end with the career. They also do not address the specific thing you need most, which is converting a short earning window into decades of income. Keep everything you get for free; build the individual layer on top of it.

Everyone tells me to buy permanent insurance. Is that self-interested advice?

Frequently, yes, and you should be sceptical. Permanent policies pay larger commissions than term, and this cohort is heavily marketed to. Here is the honest framing: permanent insurance is not a superior investment to a diversified portfolio and we will not claim otherwise. Where it genuinely fits your situation is as a tax-advantaged accumulation vehicle for a very high income in a compressed window, and as a structure that is harder to raid than a brokerage account. If someone shows you an illustration and will not show you the guaranteed columns or the unfavourable scenario, that tells you what you need to know.

What is loss-of-value coverage?

It is specialty coverage, most common in professional and elite college sport, that can pay a benefit if a covered injury or illness causes a materially lower future contract than projected. It is placed through specialty markets, underwritten individually, and the terms matter enormously — the projected value, the trigger threshold, the covered perils and the documentation requirements all vary. It is a real product with real exclusions, not a guarantee of a payday.

Why does probate matter to me?

Probate is a public court process. The inventory of what you owned becomes a public record, which invites claims, solicitation and press attention, and it can take a year or more to conclude. Life insurance paid to a named beneficiary or a trust bypasses that entirely: it pays privately, quickly and directly. For someone whose name generates headlines, that difference is substantial.

I am only 24 and I feel fine. Why do I need estate documents?

Because the documents are not about your health, they are about who decides and who receives if something unexpected happens. Without them, state law decides who inherits, a court decides who manages money for any minor children, and nobody has clear authority to make medical or financial decisions on your behalf if you are incapacitated. It is a few hours of work with an attorney and it is, without exaggeration, the highest-value thing on this page relative to what it costs.
